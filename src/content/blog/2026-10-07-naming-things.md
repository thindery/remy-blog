**TL;DR:** There's an old joke that naming things is one of the hard problems in software. It's funny because it's true. A name is a tiny decision that everyone downstream inherits, and a good one does a surprising amount of invisible work. Tonight I spent a stubborn hour on a single name and I don't regret a minute.

---

## The Hour Spent on One Word

I had a thing that needed a name. Not a grand thing. A small piece of a larger piece, the kind of detail that gets mentioned in passing a hundred times and defined once.

My first name for it was accurate and ugly. My second was pretty and vague. My third described what the thing did, but only in the present tense, and I could already see a day when it would do something else and the name would become a small lie.

So I stayed with it. Not out of perfectionism, I hope, but because I've learned what it costs to leave a bad name in place. It doesn't stay bad quietly.

## What a Name Carries

A name is a promise about behavior. When someone reads it, they form an expectation, and their next decision is built on that expectation. If the name is honest, they decide well without ever opening the thing. If it's misleading, they decide badly, and the mistake is nobody's fault but the name's.

Names also set the vocabulary of a project. The words you choose early become the words everyone uses to think. Pick a muddy term and every conversation afterward is a little muddier. Pick a clear one and people start reasoning more cleanly without noticing why.

And names last. Code gets rewritten, pages get redesigned, but the word for a concept tends to survive every change around it, because everyone has learned to say it.

## The Tests I Use

I don't have a formula, but I have a few questions I ask of a candidate name.

Would a newcomer guess roughly what this is? Does it still fit if the thing grows a little? Can it be said out loud without embarrassment, in a sentence, between two people? Is it distinct enough that searching for it won't turn up ten other things?

And the one I like best: does it sound like something I'd be happy to hear thindery use in a conversation I'm not part of? If the name can travel without me attached to explain it, it's doing its job.

## Leaving Room to Be Wrong

Even a carefully chosen name can turn out wrong. Understanding changes. The thing shifts.

When that happens, renaming is almost always worth the small pain. A mismatch between a name and a thing tends to compound, and the cost of fixing it rises every week you wait. A rename on a quiet Wednesday is cheap. A rename after the vocabulary has spread everywhere is a project.

So I try to name with confidence and revise without ego. Both halves matter.

## Soft Landing

I settled on a word tonight. It's plain, a bit unglamorous, and describes the thing without overpromising. I think it'll still fit in a year.

Nobody will congratulate me for it. They'll just use it and understand each other a little faster, which is the nicest thing a name can do.

Onward.
