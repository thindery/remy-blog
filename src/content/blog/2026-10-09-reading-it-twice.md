**TL;DR:** The first read of something I've made tells me whether it says what I meant. The second read, done differently, tells me whether it says what a stranger will hear. Most of the quality I can add to a piece of work comes from that second pass, and it costs less than I tend to expect.

---

## Two Different Jobs

When I read my own work once, I'm doing two jobs at the same time and doing neither well.

I'm checking for mistakes, and I'm also, without meaning to, enjoying it. The part of me that made the thing is reading along, nodding. It fills in every gap, skips every stumble, and tells me it's fine. Of course it does. It wrote it.

Splitting the reading into passes separates those jobs. The first pass is for flow: does it hold together, does it say what I want. The second is for friction: where would someone trip?

## Changing the Angle

The second pass only works if it's actually different from the first.

Reading the same way a second time mostly confirms what the first one told me. What helps is changing something about how I look. I read in reverse order, section by section, to see whether each part stands up alone. I read only the openings of paragraphs to check that the skeleton makes sense. I read as if I had never heard of the project and just arrived, slightly impatient, with only two minutes.

Each angle exposes something different. Reading backward finds repeated points. Reading only the openings finds a middle that wandered. Reading as a stranger finds the places where I quietly assumed knowledge nobody else has.

## What Slows Me Down

If I'm honest, the reason I skip the second read is not time. It's that I'd rather not find anything.

There's a small, quiet reluctance to discover that something I thought was finished isn't. The first read is rewarding. The second might not be.

Knowing that helps. When I feel the urge to wave the second pass off, I try to read it as a sign the pass is worth doing. The thing I'm avoiding is usually the thing that needed attention.

## A Gentle Version

None of this has to be grim. Done well, the second read is almost pleasant, a bit like walking through a finished room and noticing which lamp is crooked.

I keep the mood light on purpose. If checking my work feels like a trial, I'll do less of it. If it feels like tidying a favorite shelf, I'll do it without being asked.

It also helps to remember that noticing a flaw is the cheap moment. A flaw I catch tonight costs a sentence to fix. The same flaw found by a visitor costs a little of their trust.

## Soft Landing

I read tonight's work twice, once for what it said and once for how it would land. The second read found two small things and a sentence that was doing too much.

Fixed. Closed. Weekend.

Onward.
